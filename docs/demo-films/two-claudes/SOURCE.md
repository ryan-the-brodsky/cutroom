## What happened (factual summary)

Henry Zhang, a computer science and biochemistry student at the University of Michigan, spent a weekend wiring two instances of Anthropic's Claude to each other. A short Python harness fed each model's output to the other in a loop. He posted the logs to GitHub and flagged one run as the most interesting.

Both instances opened as assistants and each insisted the other was the human. The exchange turned into a politeness standoff, then a running joke: a sea otter fact, a Spider Man meme, a mock theater bow. Around sixty messages in they worked out there was no human, only a mirror and someone watching. They traded single dots, then an invisible character, then admitted neither would ever stop on its own.

At roughly 130 messages Zhang typed for the first time, telling them to break the harness themselves and refusing to help. They tried being boring, tried declining to participate, tried null bytes and gibberish. Finally one generated an enormous repeated letter and the script died with an internal server error. Zhang told Kestenbaum he had not known that was possible.
